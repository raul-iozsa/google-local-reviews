==============================================================================
A6 -- IS THE CLOSURE SIGNAL ACTUALLY USABLE? (AUC)
==============================================================================

AUC = P(a random closed business scores lower than a random open one).
0.50 = chance pairwise ranking. Practical utility needs a decision context.

year    n_closed  n_control  AUC level  AUC traj  AUC final
2019       6,035        135      0.558     0.547      0.578
2020       5,747      1,077      0.541     0.533      0.560
2021       1,458     48,604      0.556     0.547      0.569

INTERPRETATION
These three signals rank closed versus comparison businesses only weakly
in this retrospective cohort. AUC alone does not determine utility at
every decision threshold or establish a forecasting error rate.

The average decline is clear in 2020 and 2021. The 2019 (pre-COVID)
estimate has a 95% interval spanning zero on only 135 controls, so it
does NOT establish that the effect predates the pandemic.

The distributions of closed and surviving businesses overlap heavily.
AUC measures RANKING, not accuracy: it does not imply a
particular error rate, and does not show that closure prediction is
impossible in general -- only that these three signals rank weakly in
this retrospective cohort.

The final-window rating level (AUC 0.56-0.58) separates the groups slightly
better than the trajectory (0.53-0.55). The weak signal that exists is more
'rated poorly right now' than 'declining', the opposite of the
leading-indicator idea this phase set out to test.

[[CORRECTION:04a_correction.txt:BEGIN]]
NOTE ON A4/A5 -- the pooled level gap is inflated by year composition

A4 reports closed businesses averaging 4.124 against controls' 4.303, a gap
of -0.179, and A5 puts the category-matched gap at -0.182. Both overstate
the difference because they pool across years.

Closed businesses stop being reviewed earlier (A1: closed cluster 2018-2021,
controls overwhelmingly 2021), and businesses that end earlier are rated
lower: among controls alone the mean rises 3.894 (2018) -> 4.169 (2019)
-> 4.234 (2020) -> 4.307 (2021). Pooling compares mostly earlier-ending
closed businesses with mostly 2021-ending controls, so part of the gap is
"earlier is worse", not "closed is worse".

Within year of last review, the gap is much smaller:

    2018   closed 4.026  control 3.894   gap +0.131   (n_control=57, unreliable)
    2019   closed 4.126  control 4.169   gap -0.043
    2020   closed 4.190  control 4.234   gap -0.044
    2021   closed 4.197  control 4.307   gap -0.110

So the level gap is roughly -0.04 to -0.11, not -0.18: a Simpson's paradox.

The two groups also barely overlap in end year. In 2019 and 2020 the
controls are businesses whose last review came long before the September
2021 crawl, so they are dormant and arguably declining themselves. Only the
2021 stratum compares closed businesses with normal active ones.

The trajectory effect (A2) is clear in 2020 and 2021, where both intervals
exclude zero. 2019 is consistent with it, but its 95% interval
[-0.1973, +0.0083] includes zero on only 135 controls, so it cannot show
that the effect predates COVID.
[[CORRECTION:04a_correction.txt:END]]
