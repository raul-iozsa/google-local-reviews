NOTES ON INTERPRETING THIS COMPARISON

These scores measure agreement with star ratings, not with human judgments
of text sentiment.

The nlptown BERT model was fine-tuned elsewhere to predict product-review
stars, though not on these Google reviews. Comparing it with an in-corpus
linear model mixes architecture, pretraining and data, so similar
five-class accuracies do not show that training adds nothing. A learning
curve (one model at several training sizes) would be needed for that.

The binary comparison makes BERT choose between its combined 1-2 star and
4-5 star probabilities on the same rows as the other methods. Dropping the
cases where its top choice is 3 stars changes coverage, so that number is
not comparable.

The evaluation set is balanced by star rating, so its accuracy is not a
real-world accuracy. Positive-class recall alone says nothing about
precision, and a three-star rating need not mean neutral text.

VADER handles contrastive "but": "The staff were lovely and the room was
beautiful but the heating was broken all week" scores -0.0772. Slang and
emoji are still misread (see the taco review in the Phase 6 report).

The split has 2,735 evaluation texts that also appear in training, and
three overlapping natural review keys. On the 37,851 binary examples whose
exact text is not in training, SVM scores 93.149% and BERT 91.842%. The
ranking holds, but this is a sensitivity check, not a deduplicated, grouped
retraining. Further work: splits grouped by business, class-specific
precision and recall, macro-F1, and paired uncertainty. The human audit
(results/audit_v2) covers the separate question of agreement with human
sentiment labels.

The VADER neutral band uses positive >= 0.05 and negative <= -0.05, the same
endpoints as the human-audit scorer; four evaluation values sit exactly on
those boundaries. Coverage is checked before a report can be overwritten.
